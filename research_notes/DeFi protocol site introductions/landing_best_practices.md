# Best practices for DeFi landing pages and first-run app screens (for Fermi)

Context: Fermi is a pre-launch Uniswap v4 hook for stablecoin baskets. It runs in simulation only, has no audits, and is a hookathon entry. These notes were researched on 2026-11-19. Homepage copy was fetched live on that date and may change.

## 1. What do UX teardowns and live DeFi landing pages say about hero formulas (outcome-first vs mechanism-first)?

### Takeaway
No source tested "outcome-first" against "mechanism-first" for DeFi. The strongest framework says to match the hero to your *primary audience* and pick *one* page type: a technical spec sheet for builders, a consumer pitch for end users, or an ecosystem play only after a community exists. Leading protocols in 2026 use a short category claim ("Open infrastructure for global credit", "Digital Dollars for the Internet Economy"), followed by a one-line plain-language mechanism and a live metric.

### Cited Findings
- Dragoon ("The Protocol", May 15, 2025) defines three landing-page types. **Type 1 Technical Spec Sheet** leads with architecture and performance, has a docs-like layout and uses CTAs like "Start building" / "Read docs". **Type 2 Consumer Pitch** leads with outcomes and uses "Get started". **Type 3 Ecosystem Play** leads with mission and community stats. The advice: "pick one primary type for the homepage and commit to it", since hedging confuses every audience. Serve other audiences on separate pages, copy strategy from products of the *same* type, and do not use Type 3 too early ("start with Type 1 and move to an ecosystem page once a real community exists"). The article also says to check that the hero, the imagery and the first CTA all point to the same type — [Three Landing Page Patterns](https://designprotocol.substack.com/p/three-landing-page-patterns)
- Hero examples quoted there: Solana "Powerful for developers. Fast for everyone."; Phantom "A friendly crypto wallet"; Farcaster "A sufficiently decentralized social network" — [Three Landing Page Patterns](https://designprotocol.substack.com/p/three-landing-page-patterns)
- **Morpho** (fetched 2026-11). The hero reads "Open infrastructure for global credit". The subhead reads "Lend, borrow, and build onchain financial products with deep liquidity, competitive pricing, and control over your terms." CTAs are split by audience (Institutions / Fintechs / Users). Trust lines include "$16B+ in deposits", named builders (Coinbase, Robinhood, Société Générale), "30+ audits to date", "Built to be formally verified", "No counterparty holds your assets" and "Every line of code is fully auditable, immutable, and verifiable." — [morpho.org](https://morpho.org)
- **Ethena**. The hero reads "Digital Dollars for the Internet Economy" with the CTA "Earn with USDe". The APY carries a methodology disclaimer: it is a historical average since Jan 1 2024 and assumes no net stake or unstake during vesting. The page shows live transparency metrics (Protocol Backing Ratio, "Time below $0.997", 24/7 mint/redeem), a live view of backing assets, SOC 2 Type II and proof of reserves. There is no explicit risk-disclosure block on the homepage — [ethena.fi](https://ethena.fi)
- **Lido**. The hero reads "Liquid Decentralized Simple staking with stETH" over the subhead "Empowering and securing Ethereum since 2020", with one CTA, "Stake". Hero stats show APR, TVL (~$17.6B) and ETH staked, with footnote asterisks. A security block ("Protected and Verified") says "Over $4M invested in security, including audits, bug bounties, and expert reviews", shows auditor logos (Certora, MixBytes, Statemind, Ackee, OpenZeppelin) and links to Bug Bounty and Safe Harbor — [lido.fi](https://lido.fi)
- **Pendle**. The hero uses a rotating verb ("Trading/Hedging/Liberating/Earning…Yield") with the subhead "The world's largest crypto yield trading platform". It has a "Trusted by the best" logo strip, a metrics strip (TVL, volume, fixed yield settled), docs, education and GitHub links. The homepage shows no audits and makes the claim "no lockups, no liquidation risk" — [pendle.finance](https://www.pendle.finance)
- **Aave**. The homepage leads with a one-line definition, "Aave is a decentralized, non-custodial liquidity protocol", and keeps security on a dedicated /security page (audits, bug bounty) — [aave.com](https://aave.com)
- **Uniswap**. uniswap.org now 302-redirects to app.uniswap.org, so the "landing page" *is* the swap interface (verified 2026-11-19) — [uniswap.org](https://uniswap.org)
- Jargon-heavy heroes hurt conversion with non-experts. Beethoven-x is cited as an example of confusing hero copy — [UX of DeFi 2022 (uxdesign.cc)](https://uxdesign.cc/the-ux-of-decentralized-finance-2022-42b9fa00df4a) (2022, older than the target window)
- A fintech benchmark recommends anchoring on one benefit and then backing it with proof — [Zurb: what financial websites get wrong](https://zurb.com/blog/what-financial-websites-get-wrong-and-what-the-best-teams-do-differently)

### Inferences
- The common 2026 pattern has four parts: (1) a category or positioning noun phrase of 4–7 words, (2) a one-sentence plain-English mechanism plus benefit, (3) a single primary CTA (or audience-split CTAs), and (4) a proof strip of live numbers, audit count and named integrators. A pre-launch project like Fermi cannot honestly fill (4) with TVL or audits, so it needs substitutes (see section 3).
- Fermi's main hookathon audience is judges, integrators and LPs evaluating a mechanism. That points to **Type 1 (Technical Spec Sheet)**: a hero naming what it is (for example, "A Uniswap v4 hook for stablecoin baskets"), a subhead giving the mechanism and outcome, and CTAs "Read the spec" / "Run the simulation" rather than "Deposit" or "Earn".
- Outcome-first copy suits products that are live and consumer-facing (Ethena, Lido). Mechanism-first copy suits infrastructure (Morpho's "infrastructure", Aave's definition line). Since Fermi has no live outcome to offer, mechanism-first is also the more honest choice.

### Gaps
- No A/B test or quantitative study comparing outcome-first and mechanism-first DeFi heroes was found.
- Hyperliquid's homepage and the Uniswap Labs marketing pages were not fetched (uniswap.org redirects to the app).
- Some homepage extractions were partial, and the footnote content behind Lido's asterisks was not read.

## 2. How should pre-launch, testnet or unaudited protocols communicate their status honestly while building trust?

### Takeaway
Regulators and research agree on three rules: risk information must be **as prominent as the benefit**, it must appear **early** (not only in later screens), and safety words like "secure", "protected" or "guaranteed" should not be used unless they can be fully substantiated. For Fermi, that means a persistent, plain-language status label ("Simulation only · Unaudited · Not deployed") placed next to the hero claim, with links to what *does* exist (spec, tests, simulations).

### Cited Findings
- UK FCA FG23/3 (Nov 2023), §2.33. Promotions must avoid "unnecessary jargon". "Important information, statements or warnings should not be disguised, diminished or obscured". Promotions should "not trivialise the risks". Firms "should avoid prominently promoting the benefits without providing an equally prominent indication of the risks". They should avoid "exaggerated claims", and figures must be "accurate, up-to-date, and substantiated". Where graphics are used, they must not "give a misleading impression" — [FCA FG23/3](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/finalised-guidance/fg23-3.pdf)
- FG23/3 §2.33(g). Any indication of future performance should be "based on reasonable assumptions supported by up-to-date data" and on "performance scenarios in different market conditions (both negative and positive)". This bears directly on simulated loss-cap or return charts — [FCA FG23/3](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/finalised-guidance/fg23-3.pdf)
- FG23/3 §2.34 (citing COBS 4.2.5G). A promotion should not describe a feature as "'guaranteed', 'protected' or 'secure'" unless the term is a fair description *and* all the information needed is presented "with sufficient clarity and prominence". This applies to "loss cap"-style claims — [FCA FG23/3](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/finalised-guidance/fg23-3.pdf)
- FG23/3 §2.36. Links to fuller information are acceptable but must be prominent. "Only including information on key risks in the later stages of the financial promotion consumer journey would not be sufficient" — [FCA FG23/3](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/finalised-guidance/fg23-3.pdf)
- FG23/3 §2.48–2.51 (stablecoins). Firms must be able to demonstrate that claims of stability or a fiat link are bona fide, "by providing details of how the form of stability is maintained and any assets used". They should not use misleading terms like "inflation resistant". Stability claims for coins relying on algorithms or crypto reserves are likely breaches. The FCA's feedback statement says such coins "can create a false sense of security", citing Terra/Luna and IRON — [FCA FG23/3](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/finalised-guidance/fg23-3.pdf)
- Law-firm summary: the FCA flagged claims about "safety", "security" or ease of use, made without highlighting risk, and insufficiently visible warnings as common failures. It issued 146 alerts within a day of the regime starting (Oct 2023) — [Burges Salmon](https://www.burges-salmon.com/news-and-insight/legal-updates/fintech/crackdown-on-cryptoasset-promotions-fcas-new-guidance-for-the-industry); [Taylor Wessing 2025](https://www.taylorwessing.com/fr/interface/2025/regulating-cryptos/uk-rules-on-promoting-cryptoassets)
- Trust-signal guidance warns against unbacked claims ("most secure", "trusted by millions"), over-promising ("zero risk", "guaranteed returns") and hiding complexity. It advises acknowledging limitations, making every claim verifiable via audits, code links or visible activity, and notes "Trust compounds through repeated signals, not one-time claims." — [Trust Signals in Web3 (Jul 30, 2025)](https://designprotocol.substack.com/p/trust-signals-in-web3)
- The Uniswap Foundation's Hook Security Framework (published 1.6.2026) is voluntary and self-directed. Its principles: developer ownership, **transparency** ("teams should communicate publicly about risks"), **proportional response** (oversight scaled to hook complexity) and community evolution. Teams score risk with a worksheet in the v4 docs that maps scores to low, medium and high tiers with suggested combinations of audits, monitoring, bug bounties and formal verification. The Foundation does not certify scores — [Uniswap Foundation blog](https://uniswapfoundation.org/blog/hook-security-framework); [Uniswap v4 security docs](https://docs.uniswap.org/contracts/v4/security)
- Launch-practice sources treat audits as a gate before moving real funds ("do not deploy unaudited code that can move funds"). They recommend capped or guarded launches and a risk disclosure or "how this works" explainer that is live before users transact. Beta disclaimers in the wild use short plain lines such as "The Dapp is in beta. Use at your own risk." — [Cantina mainnet checklist](https://cantina.xyz/blog/before-you-launch-defi-mainnet-security-checklist); [MixBytes](https://mixbytes.io/blog/how-to-securely-launch-a-defi-protocol-after-an-audit-a-comprehensive-guide); [Formo](https://formo.so/blog/defi-testnet-to-mainnet-launch); [Electric Capital: Guarded Launches](https://medium.com/electric-capital/derisking-defi-guarded-launches-2600ce730e0a) (older, 2020)
- Ethena pairs its headline APY with an explicit methodology footnote (a historical average, plus its assumptions). This is a live example of qualifying a number at the point of display — [ethena.fi](https://ethena.fi)

### Inferences
- Fermi should put its status *in the hero zone*, not the footer, for example as a status chip: "Research prototype · Simulation only · Unaudited · Not deployed on mainnet". The FCA "equally prominent" test is a useful design heuristic even though a hookathon site is probably not a regulated UK financial promotion (it solicits no investment and holds no deposits).
- Avoid "loss cap", "protected", "safe" or "guaranteed" as unqualified headline words. If a bounded-loss property is the key idea, state it as a *mechanism claim with conditions*, e.g. "Designed to bound LP loss to X under [stated assumptions]; shown in simulation, not audited". Show the negative scenarios alongside the positive ones (FG23/3 §2.33(g)).
- Say "stablecoin basket" plainly and explain what holds value. Do not imply that a basket of stablecoins is itself "stable" without describing the depeg scenarios (FG23/3 §2.48–2.51).
- Self-score with the Uniswap Foundation hook framework worksheet and publish the score and tier. This is an honest, ecosystem-native status signal that exists before any audit.
- A first-run app screen for a simulator should restate the status before the first interaction, as a short modal or inline notice ("You are running a simulation. No funds move. Contracts are unaudited.").

### Gaps
- No usability study was found that measures how "unaudited" or "testnet" badges affect user trust or conversion.
- Whether FCA rules apply to a non-commercial hookathon demo is a legal question this research did not settle. FG23/3 is UK-specific; US (SEC/FTC) and EU MiCA marketing rules were not researched.
- The specific tier thresholds in the Uniswap hook framework were not retrieved (they are in the docs worksheet).

## 3. Which trust signals matter most to LPs and integrators, and what substitutes exist before launch?

### Takeaway
Users look for security information **on the project's own homepage first** and rarely go further. Audit reports are often seen as formulaic and hard to understand, so *interpretable, interactive* evidence earns more trust than an audit-logo strip. Before audits exist, substitutes such as an open-source spec, test and fuzz/invariant results, reproducible simulations, a published hook-risk self-score and explicit known limitations are the honest equivalents.

### Cited Findings
- NDSS 2025 study (20 interviews, Jun–Aug 2023, plus a review of 20 audit-firm sites and social media analysis). "All interviewees indicated that the application's homepage often serves as the initial point where they expect clear and prominent mentions of audit activities". Users "seldom venture beyond official websites" (described as "tunnel vision") — [Huang et al., NDSS 2025](https://ndss-symposium.org/wp-content/uploads/2025-775-paper.pdf)
- In the same study, users described audit reports as "hurried", "formulaic", "repetitive" and "overly simplified". Users of every experience level "frequently struggle to understand technical Web3 audit information". 38% of audit firms lack detailed process descriptions, and 62% omit auditor information entirely. Users also hold misconceptions about audit scope: some treat an audit as a code-review certificate, others as a financial audit — [NDSS 2025](https://ndss-symposium.org/wp-content/uploads/2025-775-paper.pdf)
- The study's design implications: make security information interpretable with comparative data and benchmarks, avoid overemphasising high scores without explanation (it "may undermine… credibility"), and prefer "interactive web platforms, rather than static PDF reports… side-by-side comparison tools and clickable code snippets" — [NDSS 2025](https://ndss-symposium.org/wp-content/uploads/2025-775-paper.pdf)
- About 50% of Web3 applications have had multiple audits, covering about 80% of TVL. Audits are the norm for protocols holding significant value — [NDSS 2025 / IACR 2024/1501](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1501)
- In a Radworks/Blockworks survey, 88% named connecting to malicious sites as their primary concern versus 77% for smart-contract bugs. A risk page should therefore also cover frontend and phishing risk, such as listing official domains — [Blockworks Research / Radworks](https://app.blockworksresearch.com/research/securing-de-fi-frontend-security-infrastructure-and-user-behavior-in-web3-applications). Aave does this by warning that any other app claiming to be Aave is a scam — [aave.com](https://aave.com)
- Phished MetaMask users relied on social signals such as follower counts, which "do not adequately map" to trustworthiness in Web3 — [Consensys](https://consensys.io/blog/how-and-why-are-metamask-users-losing-their-funds-due-to-phishing-incidents)
- The five trust strategies are brand recognition (investors, regulatory badges), security emphasis (audit results), transparency (open-source code, on-chain visibility, clear fees; Uniswap is the example), polish (design quality as a proxy for engineering quality) and community. The advice is to choose one primary strategy plus one secondary and not stack too many — [Trust Signals in Web3](https://designprotocol.substack.com/p/trust-signals-in-web3)
- Live examples of the evidence types now used: Morpho ("30+ audits", "built to be formally verified", "every line… verifiable"), Lido (auditor logos, $4M security spend, bug bounty, Safe Harbor), Ethena (live backing ratio, time below peg, proof of reserves, SOC 2) and Pendle (GitHub, DefiLlama and Token Terminal links) — [morpho.org](https://morpho.org); [lido.fi](https://lido.fi); [ethena.fi](https://ethena.fi); [pendle.finance](https://www.pendle.finance)

### Inferences
- For Fermi, the natural primary strategy is **transparency**, with **polish** as the secondary. Pre-launch substitutes, in rough order of how much they probably convince integrators: (1) open-source repo plus a readable spec or yellow paper; (2) a published invariant list with Foundry fuzz/invariant run counts and the commands to reproduce them; (3) an interactive simulation that lets users run stress scenarios (depeg of one basket member, liquidity shock) rather than showing static charts, matching the NDSS "interactive over PDF" finding; (4) test vectors; (5) a Uniswap hook-framework self-score; (6) a "Known limitations / what's not done" list and an "audit: not yet" line in the same place an audit badge would go.
- A "Security" or "Status" section on the homepage matters because users do not look elsewhere (NDSS). It should say plainly "No audits yet" rather than leave the section out.
- Ethena's "time below $0.997" style metric is a model for honest, falsifiable stability stats. Fermi could show the simulated basket deviation or worst-case drawdown under stated scenarios.

### Gaps
- No survey specifically of LPs or integrators (rather than general users) ranking trust signals was found.
- No evidence was found that fuzz or simulation results raise user trust; this is inference.
- The Blockworks/Radworks survey sample size was not verified (it is behind a research portal).

## 4. What are the brand voice and visual conventions in DeFi in 2025–2026, and how can Fermi stand out?

### Takeaway
The evidence base is thin and mostly comes from agency and SEO blogs. The 2026 agency consensus is a **restrained, dark-first** interface with colour reserved for status and risk, data shown live and decision-oriented, and complexity hidden but inspectable. Crypto UIs remain sans-serif dominated, so a considered serif/mono pairing, as used in research papers, is a credible point of difference, not an established convention.

### Cited Findings
- Lazarev.agency ("15 best crypto website design practices for 2026", May 14, 2026) recommends adopting "restrained, dark-first visual design", reserving colour for status, risk or primary actions and using motion sparingly. Other advice: "embed risk signals persistently", use progressive disclosure, apply a label–value–action test for widgets, explain every automatic update, and "Hide gas, routing, and solver logic by default. But never hide outcomes." It also recommends showing transaction previews with best- and worst-case outcomes — [Lazarev](https://www.lazarev.agency/articles/best-crypto-website-design)
- 2025 agency trend lists still cite neon blues, pinks and purples alongside "revised minimalism" (limited, neutral palettes) — [Merge Rocks: 10 Web3 design trends 2025](https://merge.rocks/blog/10-web3-design-trends-for-2025)
- Font-guide sites report that sans-serif (especially geometric) dominates crypto UIs, and they warn that geometric-sans plus gradient has become a cliché. They suggest serifs signal "depth and seriousness" in whitepapers. These sources are low quality and possibly auto-generated — [cryptofonts serif vs sans](https://cryptofonts-f0.pages.dev/serif-versus-sans-serif-fonts-for-crypto-marketing-cryptocurrency-typography-principles); [fontalternatives](https://fontalternatives.com/best-fonts-for/crypto-web3/)
- General (non-crypto) 2025–2026 design commentary reports a serif revival — [Design Studio UIUX typography trends](https://designstudiouiux.com/blog/typography-trends-in-web-design); [Made Good Designs 2026](https://madegooddesigns.com/logo-design-trends-2026/)
- Polish is itself a trust signal ("design quality as a proxy for engineering quality"), and consistency across the product matters — [Trust Signals in Web3](https://designprotocol.substack.com/p/trust-signals-in-web3)
- On voice, the FCA asks for no "unnecessary jargon" and no trivialising of risk, and the trust-signal guidance warns against "aggressive marketing" — [FCA FG23/3](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/finalised-guidance/fg23-3.pdf); [Trust Signals in Web3](https://designprotocol.substack.com/p/trust-signals-in-web3)

### Inferences
- Voice: precise, understated and research-lab-like ("we show, you verify") fits a pre-launch hook and fits the yellow-paper artefact. Avoid hype verbs ("revolutionary", "unbreakable", "risk-free").
- Visuals: Fermi can stand out with a light or paper-toned theme, or an editorial serif headline with mono numerics, placed against the dark-neon default. Colour should stay semantic (one accent, plus status colours for "simulated", "unaudited" and risk). Data-forward elements should be real simulation outputs with labelled assumptions.
- Common mistakes these sources point to: hedging across audience types; logo walls or stats with no verifiable link; safety words in the headline; risks only in the footer or terms; jargon-heavy heroes; static charts of best-case returns only; neon gradient plus geometric sans as an undifferentiated default; and leaving out an official-domain or anti-phishing note.

### Gaps
- No rigorous (peer-reviewed or measured) data was found on dark-mode prevalence or typography in DeFi. Claims here rest on agency opinion and live-site observation.
- No crypto rebrand case studies (for example, a move from neon to editorial styling) with outcome data were found.
