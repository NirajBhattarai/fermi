# Lead with the bound, then prove it

Comparable projects introduce themselves with a short outcome or category line, a plain-language mechanism sentence, and a proof strip of live numbers and auditor logos. The math, the risk and the competitor comparison sit one or more clicks away. No stablecoin AMM, v4 hook or math-led protocol surveyed puts a **quantified, provable bound on LP loss** in its hero. Incumbents say nothing about what happens to LPs when a pool coin fails, and Uniswap's own StablePair Hook pitches fees and spread, not a guarantee. Fermi's site does not yet introduce itself at all: the root URL opens straight onto a "Pool dashboard" with an eyebrow line ("Uniswap v4 hook · multi-stablecoin basket"), and the one sentence that says what Fermi is lives only in the HTML meta description. The fix is to add a spec-sheet style front page. It should state the T4 loss floor as a conditional number tied to its theorem, show the depeg scenario interactively, compare Fermi directly with Curve StableSwap and StablePair, and put "simulation only, unaudited, not deployed" in the hero, where an audit badge would normally go. Pre-launch, honest verification (paper, invariant tests, reproducible simulation, a published hook-risk self-score) can stand in for the TVL and audit logos Fermi cannot yet show. Users rarely look past the homepage for security information, so whatever Fermi wants believed has to be on that page.

## Incumbents sell efficiency and stay silent on failure

Stablecoin AMMs open with short superlatives that claim a category: "The Liquidity Layer For All Finance" ([Fluid](https://fluid.io/)), "The most advanced AMM ever" ([Ekubo, via search](https://ekubo.org)), "AMMs made easy" ([Balancer docs](https://docs.balancer.fi/)), "The Future Of Onchain FX" ([Mento](https://www.mento.org/)). They seldom use the word "stablecoin" in the hero. Mature brands can get away with almost nothing. Curve's root URL renders only a logo splash and a spinner, and its meta tag reads like engineering documentation: "Curve-frontend is a user interface application designed to connect to Curve's deployment of smart contracts" ([curve.finance](https://www.curve.finance/)). A new entrant has no such brand equity. Mento's page is the useful template: hero, product cards, **stat tiles (12M+ users, $18.5B 2025 volume, 15 stablecoins, 3:1 collateralization)**, build/integrate, "Building Trust with Transparency", named auditors with dates, then blog and newsletter ([mento.org](https://www.mento.org/)).

Risk gets far less space than efficiency. Fluid relegates risk to footer links ("Bug Bounty", "Transparency", "Risk Research") ([fluid.io](https://fluid.io/)). Balancer's and PancakeSwap's stable-pool docs have no depeg warnings ([Balancer](https://docs.balancer.fi/concepts/explore-available-balancer-pools/stable-pool/stable-pool.html); [PancakeSwap](https://docs.pancake.xyz/trade/stableswap)). Curve's user introduction mentions losses only for LLAMMA liquidations ([Curve docs](https://docs.curve.finance/user/introduction)). Independent analysts describe the real risk the landing pages leave out. During the March 2023 USDC depeg, **Curve slippage rose above 3% as the pool became heavily imbalanced**, and a stable pool's main failure mode is LPs ending up holding the failing coin ([Spark comparison](https://www.spark.money/tools/stablecoin-automated-market-maker-comparison)). The silence has an obvious cause: in a StableSwap pool, LPs absorb the failed coin, and that does not sell. So the question every multi-stablecoin LP should ask ("what happens to me if one coin dies?") goes unanswered on every major landing page.

Uniswap's StablePair Hook, launched in September 2026, is now the default comparison for Fermi. It uses a two-audience line ("gives traders consistent, predictable quotes on every swap, and LPs a bigger share of the value they create") and explains its mechanism through three plain cases: a fixed spread near peg, no fee for trades moving away from the reference price, and a Dutch-auction fee that "starts high and falls with each block" for trades moving back ([The Block](https://theblock.co/news/defi/2026-09-10-uniswap-stablepair-hook-414160)). It covers **pairs** (USDC/USDT, USDC/USDG). Its LP promise is qualitative, and the launch coverage mentions neither Curve nor depegs. Balancer, for its part, documents a "safe maximum of 5 tokens, due to the constraints of Stable Math" for stable pools ([Balancer docs](https://docs.balancer.fi/concepts/explore-available-balancer-pools/stable-pool/stable-pool.html)). That leaves the N-coin basket with a stated tail-risk cap unclaimed.

## Hook projects lead with the outcome and mention v4 third

Successful v4 hook projects almost never open with "we are a hook". Doppler's hero is "Token launches that work.", followed by a three-verb how-it-works ("Create the market → Let price form → Graduate liquidity"). It splits its CTAs by audience: "Launch a token" for users, "Start building" and "Read the docs" for integrators, and "Read the paper" for researchers ([doppler.lol](https://www.doppler.lol)). Bunni's docs overview mentions neither "hook" nor "v4" and anchors on something readers already know ("Building upon the concept of ticks from Uniswap v3") ([Bunni docs](https://docs.bunni.xyz/docs/v2/overview)). Angstrom names the LP problem in everyday terms ("Traditional AMMs leak immense value to arbitrageurs") before using the term LVR, and it splits its benefits into For Liquidity Providers / For Traders / For Token Issuers ([Angstrom docs](https://docs.angstrom.xyz)). EulerSwap, the closest architectural analogue to Fermi as a custom curve in a hook, tells traders the hook stays out of sight ("unaware that beneath the surface…"). It tells integrators that the hook "can plug into Uniswap's routing and solver networks" ([Euler blog, via search](https://www.euler.finance/blog/introducing-eulerswap)). Bunni's README now opens with "Bunni v2 has been exploited" ([GitHub](https://github.com/bunniapp/bunni-v2)), a reminder that a security event outweighs any hero copy and that judges and LPs are primed to look for invariants.

Hookathon evaluation rewards claims a judge can check. No published UHI rubric exists. ETHGlobal's Uniswap Foundation prize asks for a public repo and says "make sure your README clearly points to the relevant contracts and lines of code so we can verify your integration" ([ETHGlobal Tokyo 2026](https://ethglobal.com/events/tokyo2026/prizes/uniswap-foundation)). In UHI9's Reactive track, judges checked whether each project's contract **actually fired a callback on-chain**, and only six passed ([Reactive blog](https://blog.reactive.network/reactive-contracts-uniswap-hooks-the-first-wave-from-the-uhi9-hookathon/)). The same write-up gives every project a short problem-to-solution subtitle ("A Risk Dial That Turns Itself"), and many UHI9 entries frame themselves around LP loss. UHI Demo Day panels include investors from a16z, Variant, Dragonfly and USV ([Luma, via search](https://lu.ma/0p6uabf2)), so the pitch is also judged as an investable product. Strong ETHGlobal showcase pages include a labelled component breakdown, an explicit safety claim ("the hook never performs external calls"), and a read-only "verification & observability" dashboard ([StableFlow](https://ethglobal.com/showcase/stableflow-v4sbe)). Fermi already has the dashboard. What it lacks is the one-line problem statement and the verification pointers.

## Math-led protocols turn theorems into negations and keep proofs one click away

Protocols built on serious math keep formulas out of the hero. They express the math as an identity or a structural negation that is literally true of the code. Panoptic writes "Your AMM liquidity is the option" and "No order books. No oracles. No expiries." ([panoptic.xyz](https://panoptic.xyz/)). Ajna uses "No permission | No governance | No PRICE FEEDS" ([ajna.finance](https://www.ajna.finance/)). Liquity v2 says "cannot be changed or upgraded" ([liquity.org](https://www.liquity.org/)). Economic claims get hedged or scoped. Uniswap v3 paired "up to 4000x capital efficiency" with "taking on more price risk" in the same announcement ([Uniswap blog](https://blog.uniswap.org/uniswap-v3)), and Curve's LLAMMA docs limit losses to when "the loan is in liquidation mode" ([Curve docs, via search](https://docs.curve.finance/docs/developer/crvusd/overview.md)). No surveyed homepage ties a headline claim to a numbered theorem. Fermi can do that, and it would set Fermi apart.

Fermi's theorem fits this structure unusually well, because it is a precise conditional claim rather than a promise of yield. Yellow paper T4's single-failure corollary states that **if coin *h* goes to zero, LP loss ≤ w_h·V·(1 + b_h) + ε₀**, a bound that "does not depend on the sequence of swaps, on the starting skew, or on how much of the coins in S was minted" and is tight in the limit (`docs/yellow-paper.md`, §4). It turns into numbers a visitor can grasp. With equal weights and a 30% budget, a 3pool-sized basket caps a one-coin failure at **⅓ × 1.3 ≈ 43%**. In the paper's own vector, a young coin at 6.25% weight with a 10% budget caps it at **≈ 6.9%**. The headline can therefore carry a parameter, a condition and a citation, the same discipline Uniswap and Curve applied to their own numbers.

Papers tend to come in two depths and carry a version label. Panoptic publishes a litepaper and a whitepaper on separate subdomains ([panoptic.xyz](https://panoptic.xyz/)). Ajna offers an ELI5 PDF alongside a whitepaper with a dated filename ([ajna.finance](https://www.ajna.finance/)). Liquity labels "rev. 0.3 (November, 2024)" ([liquity.org](https://www.liquity.org/)). Balancer's math pages follow a pattern Fermi should copy: formula, then variable definitions, then a caveat callout, then reference implementations in TypeScript and Python ([Balancer Weighted Math](https://docs.balancer.fi/concepts/explore-available-balancer-pools/weighted-pool/weighted-math.html)). For credibility, Primitive's arXiv preprints with named authors and a public simulations repo ([arXiv 2310.14320](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.14320)) carry more academic weight than a PDF hosted on a project site. Interactive math is rare. The strongest example found, Panoptic's "Your Uniswap LP Is a Short Option (Interactive Charts)", is promoted from its homepage ([panoptic.xyz](https://panoptic.xyz/)). No stablecoin AMM shows a "fail a coin" simulation anywhere.

## Pre-launch honesty belongs in the hero, not the footer

Landing-page guidance points to one page type. Dragoon's framework separates a Technical Spec Sheet (architecture first, "Start building / Read docs"), a Consumer Pitch (outcomes, "Get started") and an Ecosystem Play (mission and community stats). It advises picking one and committing to it, and starting with the spec sheet until a real community exists ([Design Protocol](https://designprotocol.substack.com/p/three-landing-page-patterns)). Fermi's audience is judges, integrators and sophisticated LPs evaluating a mechanism, and it has no live outcome to sell, so it is a spec sheet. An outcome-led hero would promise returns no one can collect yet.

Trust research makes the homepage the place where trust is decided. In an NDSS 2025 interview study, **all interviewees said the homepage is where they expect clear, prominent mention of audits, and users "seldom venture beyond official websites"**. The same users found audit reports "formulaic" and hard to read, and the authors recommend "interactive web platforms, rather than static PDF reports… side-by-side comparison tools and clickable code snippets" ([Huang et al., NDSS 2025](https://ndss-symposium.org/wp-content/uploads/2025-775-paper.pdf)). Audits are the norm for protocols holding real value: about half of Web3 apps have had several audits, covering about 80% of TVL ([IACR 2024/1501](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1501)). An audit section that simply disappears therefore looks like concealment. Saying "Audit: not yet" in that spot reads as candour. Frontend risk matters to users too: **88% named connecting to malicious sites as their top concern, against 77% for contract bugs** ([Blockworks Research / Radworks](https://app.blockworksresearch.com/research/securing-de-fi-frontend-security-infrastructure-and-user-behavior-in-web3-applications)). Phishing clones of Curve and Balancer turned up throughout this research, which makes an official-links note worth adding.

The UK FCA's crypto promotion guidance sets a useful design rule, even though a hookathon demo that solicits no deposits is probably outside its scope. Risk must be **as prominent as the benefit**, must appear early rather than only "in the later stages of the… consumer journey", and words such as "guaranteed", "protected" or "secure" are acceptable only when they are fair and fully qualified. Forward-looking figures should show "performance scenarios in different market conditions (both negative and positive)" ([FCA FG23/3](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/finalised-guidance/fg23-3.pdf)). Applied to Fermi, the floor should read as "loss is capped at w(1+b) of the pool", never "LPs are protected". The simulator should show the bad case as clearly as the calm one. The ecosystem also offers a native status signal: the Uniswap Foundation's voluntary Hook Security Framework asks teams to "communicate publicly about risks" and score their hook with a worksheet that maps to low, medium or high tiers and suggests the matching audits, monitoring and formal verification ([Uniswap Foundation](https://uniswapfoundation.org/blog/hook-security-framework); [v4 security docs](https://docs.uniswap.org/contracts/v4/security)). Publishing that self-score is a step no surveyed competitor has taken.

On voice and visuals, the evidence is weaker, mostly agency opinion. That opinion favours a restrained interface, colour reserved for status and risk, and "never hide outcomes" ([Lazarev](https://www.lazarev.agency/articles/best-crypto-website-design)), and it treats design polish as a stand-in for engineering quality ([Design Protocol](https://designprotocol.substack.com/p/trust-signals-in-web3)). A calm, research-lab voice that names the risk and then bounds it stands out against a field of terse superlatives. So does an editorial paper-toned look against neon gradients. Fermi's existing "Simulation" chip and yellow-paper tab already lean this way.

## Recommended changes to Fermi's site, in priority order

The changes below assume the current React app (`web/src/App.tsx`), which defaults to the Dashboard tab. The main structural move is a new default **Overview** route that introduces Fermi before the dashboard, keeping the dashboard one click away as the proof. The numbers come from the yellow paper and the existing presets. Each one should be shown next to its parameters.

### Hero copy options

| Option | Headline | Subhead | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| A (recommended) | **Stablecoin baskets with a floor.** | A Uniswap v4 hook for multi-stablecoin pools. If any one coin fails, LP loss is capped at that coin's weight × (1 + budget), fixed at deployment and proved in the yellow paper (Theorem T4). | Judges, LPs; keeps the existing `<title>` and tagline |
| B | **When a stablecoin fails, your loss has a ceiling.** | Fermi pools quote near 1:1 like Curve, but each coin hits a wall past its budget, so a dead coin can drain at most w(1+b) of the pool. In a 3pool-style basket, that is about 43%. | LP-first framing; boldest, needs the condition visible |
| C | **One deposit, many stablecoins, bounded tail risk.** | A v4 hook with its own Fermi–Dirac pricing curve: near-constant-sum below each coin's wall, a hard cap on what any failed coin can take. | Integrators; closest to Doppler and EulerSwap style |

Directly under every option, at the same visual weight as the subhead, place a status line: **"Research prototype · Simulation only · Unaudited · Not deployed."** Avoid "safe", "protected", "guaranteed" and "no loss" anywhere in the hero.

## Conclusion

Fermi's strongest asset is a claim that the competitive field cannot make: a deployment-time number for the worst case. Everyone else competes on the calm case, with capital efficiency, tighter quotes and "a bigger share of the value". The landing page should therefore invert the usual hierarchy and present the failure scenario as the main attraction rather than a disclaimer. Being honest about status is part of what makes that claim credible. A project that boxes its theorem, names its conditions and writes "Audit: not yet" where others place logos is practising on its own site the bounded, verifiable approach the protocol is built on.

Three things in this evidence remain uncertain. No study measures whether "unaudited" badges or simulators change LP or judge behaviour. No UHI scoring rubric is public. Live hero copy for Curve, Balancer, Ekubo and Maverick could not be read. The recommendations rest on consistent patterns and stated principles, not conversion data. The main open risk is positional: Uniswap's StablePair Hook owns the plain phrase "stablecoin pools where LPs keep more". If it extends to multi-asset pools or adds depeg handling, Fermi's edge narrows to the provable bound alone. That is another reason to make the bound, and the evidence behind it, the first thing a visitor sees.
